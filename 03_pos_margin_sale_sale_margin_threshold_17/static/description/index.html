How it works

From a cost price to a stopped confirmation, in five steps

There is no rules engine here, no approval matrix and no new menu to learn. One percentage, one computed floor, and one test that runs at the exact moment a quotation is about to become an order — with one setting, Blocking Transaction Order, deciding whether that test asks or refuses.

1

A margin, set once

Type a percentage into Margin on a product category. Every product inside it inherits the number; individual templates override it when they need to.

2

A floor, computed

Minimum sale price is cost × (1 + margin) — a cost of 100 at 25% gives a floor of 125. When the cost moves, the floor moves with it. Type the floor directly and the margin is worked out backwards.

3

A line that goes red

On the quotation, a unit price below that product's floor renders red in the order lines — visible while the rep is still typing, not after anyone presses anything.

4

The test at Confirm

Confirming the order runs every line's unit price against its own floor and collects the products that fall short, each with the amount it is short by.

5

Ask, or refuse

Ask mode opens a dialog listing each under-priced product and its floor; the rep confirms anyway or cancels. Refuse mode raises a validation error instead and the order stays a quotation.

Features

Twelve ways it puts a minimum sale price into Odoo 17 quotations

All of it lands in the screens your sales team already uses — the product form, the quotation line, the Confirm button. There is no new app to learn, no approval matrix to design, and nothing to switch on beyond one percentage.

Set the margin once, on the category

Type one percentage into Margin on a product category and every product inside it gets a price floor. Eleven categories instead of two thousand products.

The floor follows your cost price

Minimum sale price is cost × (1 + margin), recomputed the moment a supplier puts your cost up. Nobody has to remember to revisit a number.

Or type the floor and let the margin follow

Already know the price you will not go below? Type it in and the margin percentage is worked out backwards. The field is computed and writable, so both directions work.

Under-priced lines turn red on the quotation

The unit price renders red in the order lines as soon as it drops below that product's floor — while the rep is still negotiating, not after the deal is done.

Confirm names every product that is short

The message lists each offending line with its minimum price in your own currency, so the rep knows exactly what to re-price instead of hunting through the order.

Reps can still override, on purpose

Ask mode lets them confirm anyway in one click. Judgement stays with the person closing the deal — they simply cannot exercise it by accident any more.

Or make the price floor absolute

Refuse mode raises a validation error and the document stays a quotation until a line is re-priced. There is no override anywhere in the interface.

One switch decides which one you get

Blocking Transaction Order in Sales settings moves the whole team between asking and refusing. Nothing is stored per order, so you can change your mind.

The warning speaks your rep's language

Written in English and French, picked from the user's own Odoo language. The rest of the module ships in six: English, French, Arabic, Indonesian, Portuguese and Spanish.

Rental orders are left alone

With Odoo Rental installed, rental orders confirm with no margin check at all. Rental pricing follows its own logic and this module does not argue with it.

Set margins in bulk from the product list

Tick any group of products, run Update margin sale, and apply one percentage across all of them — for the exceptions that never map cleanly onto a category.

Runs alongside the Point of Sale module

Install POS Margin Threshold too and the product form still shows one set of margin fields rather than two. The bulk action de-duplicates itself as well.

Rolling it out

The first thirty minutes, if you want to be careful about it

That is the whole module. A price floor is a rule about people's judgement, though, so it is worth introducing narrow and quiet rather than all at once — and because it is free, the cheapest way to find out whether this is the rule you want is to run it and read what it catches.

01

Install it, then set a margin on exactly one category

Drop the folder in your addons path, update the apps list, install. Then pick a single product category — ideally one where you already suspect the discounting is thin — and put a percentage in its Margin field. Every other product in the database is untouched and behaves exactly as it did this morning.

02

Leave it in ask mode and say nothing dramatic to the team

Blocking Transaction Order is off by default, so nothing is ever blocked. Reps confirm quotations the way they always have; the only new thing is a dialog when a line sits under a floor, and they can accept it and carry on. Tell them what it means, not that they are being measured.

03

Watch what it catches for a fortnight

The interesting number is not how many deals were bad — it is how often the dialog appeared at all. Constant firing usually means the floor is set higher than your real pricing; complete silence means it is set below anything anyone would ever type. Either way you have learned where your policy actually sits, and nobody has been stopped from selling.

04

Then widen it, tune it, or make it absolute

Three moves are available and each is one field or one switch: add margins to more categories, adjust the percentage on the one you started with, or turn Blocking Transaction Order on and make the floor genuinely non-negotiable. Orders you already confirmed are never revisited.

There is no trial clock, so there is no hurry

Nothing expires, nothing phones home and nothing counts your users, so a pilot can run as long as you want it to. If you decide against the idea, uninstalling takes the fields and the setting away and leaves every product, price, quotation and order exactly as it was — there is nothing in your data that depends on this module continuing to exist.

FAQ

Thirteen real questions, answered plainly

Every answer comes from the module's source and a clean Odoo 17.0 test run.

Does it block the order, or just warn about it?

Whichever you choose. With Blocking Transaction Order off — the default — Confirm opens a dialog listing the under-priced products and the rep can confirm anyway or cancel. With it on, Confirm raises a validation error and the order stays a quotation.

How is the minimum price worked out?

Cost price times one plus the margin. Cost 100 with a 25% margin gives a floor of 125. You can also type the floor directly and the margin percentage is derived from it.

Do I have to fill in a margin on every product?

No. Set it on the product category and everything inside inherits it. Override the individual exceptions, and use the bulk Update margin sale action for a group that does not match a category cleanly.

Which cost does it compare against?

The product's standard cost field, which your own costing method maintains. If you run average cost, the floor follows your average cost. The module does not impose a costing method.

Does the check use the price before or after tax?

The confirmation check compares the line's unit price against Minimum sale price, which is the pre-tax figure — the same basis as the unit price on the quotation line, so it is comparing like with like. The tax-inclusive figure is also computed on the product and shown there for reference.

Can I confirm several quotations at once from the list view?

Not with this module installed, and this is a defect rather than a design choice. The check is written for one order at a time, so a multi-order confirmation raises an Expected singleton error instead of confirming. Confirm orders one at a time from the order form and everything works. It is ours, we found it in our own test run, and the fix is first on the list in Releases below.

Can two variants of the same product have different margins?

Not today, and this is the second limit we would rather you heard from us. The margin field appears on product variants, but writing it on one variant writes to the shared product template, so the other variants follow it. If your variants have genuinely different costs and need genuinely different margins, this module does not give you that yet — it is on the list in Releases. Template-level margins work exactly as described.

What happens to rental orders?

They are skipped entirely and confirm as normal. The module checks whether the order is a rental order and, if it is, hands straight back to Odoo without running the margin test. That is deliberate, not an oversight.

Will it fight with my pricelists or discounts?

No. Pricelists and discounts still decide the price. The floor is a separate test applied to whatever unit price ends up on the line, however it got there.

Can I install this and the POS module together?

Yes, and they are designed for it. They share the same product fields, and this module hides its duplicate copies and steps back from the bulk action when POS Margin Threshold is present. You get one set of margin fields, not two.

Is the warning translated?

The message is written in English and French: a user whose Odoo language starts with fr reads French, everyone else reads English. The rest of the module's labels also ship in Arabic, Indonesian, Portuguese and Spanish.

Does anything about my costs or prices leave my server?

Nothing. The module makes no outbound network call of any kind — no telemetry, no licence check, no cloud component. There is nothing for us to switch off.

What happens if I uninstall it?

The margin and minimum-price fields, the setting and the confirmation dialog go away. Your products, prices, costs, quotations and sale orders stay exactly as they were. Nothing in your data depends on the module continuing to exist.
